If there's one shot that separates players who can rally from players who
can *win points*, it's the third shot drop. Here's how we teach it in
private lessons and clinics.

## Why the third shot matters

After the serve and return, the serving team is stuck back at the baseline
while the receiving team is already at the non-volley zone line. The third
shot drop is how the serving team neutralizes that disadvantage and earns
their way forward.

Skip the drop and go for a hard drive instead, and you're gambling against
two opponents who are already set up at the kitchen with the advantage.

## The three things we drill first

1. **Paddle angle before speed.** Most missed drops come from players
   trying to generate pace instead of trusting a soft, open paddle face.
2. **Bend your knees, not your wrist.** Power for the drop should come from
   your legs pushing up through the shot, not a wristy flick.
3. **Aim for arrival, not the net.** We tell players to picture the ball
   landing softly in the kitchen, not just clearing the net &mdash; that
   shift in target completely changes contact.

## A simple drill to start today

Stand at the baseline with a partner (or coach) feeding balls from the
kitchen. Hit 10 drops focusing only on paddle angle and a soft push from
the legs. Don't worry about where it lands yet &mdash; just get comfortable
with the motion. Once that feels natural, start tracking how many land in
the kitchen out of 10.

> "Most players think the third shot drop is about touch. It's actually
> about patience &mdash; the touch comes once the patience is there."
