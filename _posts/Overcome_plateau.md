Stuck on a plateau? You're not alone.
If you've been playing a while and feel like you've hit a wall — same games, same mistakes — that's a plateau. And lately a lot of us have been saying the same thing: just playing rec games isn't enough to break through a plateau. 📈
A real growth plan mindset first. That plan should include three simple elements, each built through targeted drills and targeted games.
🧘 Patience — mostly about shot selection and resisting the urge to force the wrong ball.
🎯 Opportunities awareness or creating — built by drilling triangle patterns, court positioning, and reading opponents or your partner, so you can set up instead of just reacting.
🔁 Consistency — built through drills for eye tracking, footwork, etc., so your contact point and balance hold up under pressure.
But here's the catch: you can't build those three on rec games alone. Why not? Because most rec play lands at one of two extremes — and neither is where growth actually happens. The science (to be posted soon here 👀 — stay tuned!) explains why:
⬇️ Too easy rec games → you slip into "competitive mode," only hitting your safe shots and protecting your rating. Comfortable, but it's exactly how plateaus form.
🔥 Too hard → pure chaos. The ball comes back too fast, errors pile up, and you walk off having learned nothing but frustration.
⭐ The sweet spot → around a 70% success rate. You win enough to stay confident, but lose enough (that other 30%) to force real growth. That's where plateaus finally break.
So the real question isn't "how much should I play?" — it's "how do I spend more time in that sweet spot?" That's where a plan, and the right environment, make all the difference.
