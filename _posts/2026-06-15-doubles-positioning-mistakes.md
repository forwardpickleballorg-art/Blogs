title: "5 Doubles Positioning Mistakes We See in Every Clinic"
category: Doubles
author: Coach Team
---

Doubles pickleball is won and lost on positioning as much as it is on
shot-making. In our group clinics, these five mistakes come up in almost
every session &mdash; regardless of skill level.

## 1. Standing side-by-side instead of moving as a unit

Your team should move together like it's connected by an invisible rope.
When your partner shifts to cover a sideline shot, you should be sliding
with them to protect the middle &mdash; not standing still.

## 2. Splitting the middle instead of calling it

The "middle ball" hesitation is one of the most common ways teams lose
easy points. Before the point even starts, decide who takes middle balls on
the forehand and who takes them on the backhand, and communicate out loud
during the point.

## 3. Crashing the kitchen line too early

Rushing forward before your team has actually earned the transition (with
a solid drop or dink) leaves you exposed to a driven ball at your feet.
Move up in the same rhythm as your shot quality improves.

## 4. Standing too far back at the non-volley zone line

Once you're at the kitchen, hovering a foot or two behind the line gives
opponents extra time and angle on their attacks. Get right up to the line
(without crossing it on a volley) so you can take balls out of the air.

## 5. Not resetting after a poach

If your partner poaches a ball, the court is temporarily uncovered. Both
players need a habit of immediately resetting to standard positioning
after any poach or defensive scramble.

## Try this in your next open play session

Pick one mistake from this list and make it your only focus for a full
session. Positioning habits change through repetition, not through trying
to fix everything at once.
