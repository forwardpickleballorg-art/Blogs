title: "What We Teach in Every Beginner's First Lesson"
category: Beginners
author: Coach Team
---

New students often expect their first lesson to start with fast-paced
rallies. Instead, we spend almost the entire first session on three things
that make every future lesson easier to build on.

## 1. The ready position

Before we talk about any shot, we make sure a player's paddle is up,
knees are bent, and weight is on the balls of their feet between shots.
Almost every rushed or mishit ball traces back to a player caught flat-
footed with the paddle down.

## 2. The continental-ish grip

We don't obsess over textbook grip names in lesson one &mdash; we care
that a player can hit a forehand and backhand without needing to
re-grip between shots. That single adjustment removes a huge source of
inconsistency for brand-new players.

## 3. Dinking, before anything else

It feels counterintuitive to spend lesson one dinking instead of driving,
but soft-game control is what keeps rallies alive long enough to learn
everything else. We'd rather a beginner leave lesson one able to sustain a
10-shot dink rally than hit one impressive winner and net the next five.

## Why we start here

Pickleball rewards consistency far more than power, especially at the
levels most new players are trying to reach first (3.0&ndash;3.5). Starting
with ready position, a comfortable grip, and soft hands means every lesson
after the first one can focus on strategy instead of re-teaching
fundamentals.
